# Open Problem 4: A New Problem, You Are in the Wrong Place

Everyday Workplace Communication · Module 1 · Segment 5: Reading Signs, The Wrong Room · **Stage 5.7** (COM-M1-OP-04), in the Swift AI Academy frame.

The whole activity is one plain HTML page (`index.html`), with its pictures and sounds inside the file. No framework or build step is needed. `Module1_Open_Problem_4_Stage5.7_You_Are_in_the_Wrong_Place.html` is the same file, kept for downloading. It opens with a double-click.
The page is generated from `module1-segment1/` with `node scripts/plain-page.mjs scripts/op57-plain.template.html <out.html>`. Do not edit it by hand.

## Publish on GitHub + Vercel

1. In Vercel choose **Add New → Project** and import this repository.
2. Set **Framework Preset** to **Other**. Leave the build command and output directory empty.
3. Choose **Deploy**.

GitHub Pages also works: Settings → Pages → deploy from the `main` branch, root folder.

## How it works

1. **Your first move.** A short animated story opens the activity. It is set mid-morning in the empty Room 2, with stacked plastic chairs, dusty light, a blank board, and an open door onto the corridor. Ananya walks in by mistake, looking for Room 12, and says "Oh. This is not Room 12." The board on the door is highlighted: it says Room 2. The story ends: "You are in the wrong room. What do you do first?". The narrator reads each caption as it types in. It plays once, on its own where the browser allows sound. Browsers block sound until the visitor taps or presses a key, so otherwise the story runs silently, a "Tap to hear the story" button shows, and the first tap starts it again from the beginning with sound. People who prefer reduced motion see the final picture. Then the script's question: "You realise you are in the wrong room right now, and no one is around yet. What do you do first?" The learner writes their first move, and why (5 to 50 words).
2. **Compare and reflect.** The learner sees their answer and a short feedback message. The message is built from an internal check: a sensible first move (several are accepted), a reason, and a finished sentence ("I would ask" must say who). **The key words and the checks are never shown to learners**; staff can find them in the separate checking-metric document. Then come the brief's three small cards: go back the way you came, read the signs again, and look for someone to ask. They are labelled as ideas to compare, not a correction. Optional voice practice has a Practice badge and a consent tick box; it is recorded in the browser only and never checked. The learner can choose "Try another answer".
3. **Take it with you.** "Write the exact words you would say to someone, to find Room 12." (3 to 30 words). The feedback message comes from an internal check of five points: polite words, polite words first, a question, "could you" followed by what you need, and which room (Room 12). Then three ways to say it, to compare, from Segment 7.3.

At the end: "Practice complete", **Download my notes** (a text file) and **Delete my saved work**.

- **Not graded:** a "Practice · not graded" badge and no score. Attempts are saved in the browser as `5.7.open-problem-log`. Nothing is sent anywhere; recordings are never saved or uploaded.
- The top bar shows "About 10 minutes" and never counts down. There is no Pause button and no time limit, as in the other Module 1 pages.
- No contractions are used anywhere on screen.
